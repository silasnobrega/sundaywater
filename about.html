About Sunday

Clean water shouldn't be complicated.

Sunday started with a simple frustration: why should homeowners have to question whether the water coming out of their own faucets is safe to drink?

As a Connecticut resident, I've experienced that uncertainty myself. You turn on the tap, fill a glass, and wonder what's actually in it. Is it safe? Should I be filtering it? What kind of system do I even need?

And when you start looking for answers, things only seem to get more complicated.

Between confusing water quality reports, countless filtration products, technical terminology, and expensive systems, it's difficult to know who to trust or where to begin.

I started Sunday because I believe there should be a better way.

Why we do this

Honestly, we got into this because of what we learned about the rules, not the water.

Our drinking water standards are mostly from the seventies and nineties. In 2024 the EPA finally admitted that forever chemicals (PFAS) are in the tap water of close to 200 million Americans and set the first legal limits for six of them4,6. Then it gave utilities until 2029 to fix it.

Then it pushed that to 2031, and took four of the six chemicals off the list entirely7.

So nobody is coming to fix it at the plant any time soon. The only place you can actually control what’s in your water is your own tap, and most people don’t know that.

That’s why we do this.

Forever chemicals, in plain English

PFAS are man-made chemicals used since the 1940s in non-stick pans, waterproof coatings, firefighting foam and food packaging. They don’t break down, so they build up in water, soil and people. These are the six the EPA set out to limit in 2024, and what each has been linked to8,9:

PFOA Teflon, stain-proof fabric
Kidney and testicular cancer, high cholesterol, liver damage, weaker response to vaccines, high blood pressure in pregnancy, lower birth weight. Classified as carcinogenic to humans in 2023.
PFOS Scotchgard, firefighting foam
Weakened immune system, high cholesterol, liver and thyroid effects, lower birth weight. Classified as possibly carcinogenic in 2023.
PFHxS Firefighting foam, carpets
Thyroid disruption, liver effects, weakened immune response. Dropped from the federal limits.
PFNA Food packaging, textiles
Lower birth weight and developmental effects, liver damage, higher cholesterol, immune effects. Dropped from the federal limits.
GenX (HFPO-DA) The “safer” replacement for PFOA
Liver and kidney damage, immune and developmental effects in animal studies. Dropped from the federal limits.
PFBS Replacement for PFOS
Thyroid, kidney and reproductive effects in animal studies. Dropped from the federal limits.

You can’t see, taste or smell any of them. A utility report tells you what left the plant. A test at your sink tells you what you’re drinking. And reverse osmosis at the kitchen tap is the stage that takes them out.

A simpler approach to clean water

My goal was to build a company that makes water treatment straightforward, affordable, and accessible to everyday homeowners.

  • No confusing explanations.
  • No unnecessary equipment.
  • No high-pressure sales tactics.

Just honest answers, easy-to-understand solutions, and quality systems installed by professionals who care about doing the job right.

Whether you're concerned about contaminants, unpleasant tastes and odors, hard water, or simply want to better understand what's coming out of your faucet, we're here to help you make an informed decision.

Because you shouldn't need to become a water treatment expert to feel confident about the water in your home.

More than a water company

I believe businesses have an opportunity to do real good—not only for their customers, but for their employees and the communities around them.

I want Sunday to be a place where people are challenged to grow, equipped with valuable skills, and encouraged to become leaders. A company that doesn't simply create jobs, but creates opportunities.

My Christian faith has shaped much of that vision. I believe that what we're given comes with a responsibility to steward it well, serve others, and use our success for something beyond ourselves.

While we help families here in Connecticut enjoy better water at home, I want our work to help families across the world gain access to something so many of us take for granted.
